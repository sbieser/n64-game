# Stage 9 — The Rogue

*No star. No sun. No light from above. This world has been alone in the dark for billions of years.*

---

## Setting

A rogue planet — ejected from its solar system an immense time ago, now drifting through interstellar space with no star to orbit. The surface has no sunlight. The sky is not the black of the void with its field of navigating stars: the stars are there, impossibly distant, the Milky Way a faint smear, but no single star close enough to matter. This is a different quality of darkness from anything the player has encountered. The void was empty space between things. This is a world that exists in darkness, has always existed in darkness, will always exist in darkness.

Beneath the surface: residual formation heat and slow radioactive decay keep a thin region warmer than absolute zero. The planet is not dead. It is alone.

The player navigates across a dark surface lit only by their ship's lights. The lights reach perhaps twenty meters. Beyond that: dark.

---

## Mode

Free-roam. The scale of the surface is planetary but the player navigates a bounded region — not bounded by walls but by thermal necessity. Moving too far from the warm zone risks hull freeze. The warm zone is detectable but poorly localized: warmth radiating from below through the surface, diffuse, gradient rather than point source.

---

## Threat — Cold

Hull temperature, as on the ice moon. But without clear vent points to seek. The warmth here radiates from below, through the surface, everywhere slightly and more warm in some directions than others. Gradient navigation: the seeking mechanic for warmth, but diffuse — more like reading the temperature of a large room than following a vent.

This stage is slower and more patient than the ice moon. The ice moon had sharp vent points. Here the signal for warmth is everywhere and subtle. The player develops sensitivity to small differences.

See `mechanics/oxygen.md` for the planetary equivalent table. See `mechanics/ghosts.md` for thermal charge resources.

---

## Seeking

The cosmic signal is detectable here — no atmosphere to block it, no significant magnetosphere to attenuate it. The signal is present and clear in direction.

But finding direction is harder than in the void. Stage 1's void had stars to triangulate against, a field of visual context. The rogue planet has only ground and horizon and the twenty-meter radius of ship light. Direction must be established from the signal itself, with no visual reference to anchor it.

**Stop → Rotate → Listen → Establish heading from signal alone → Thrust**

This is the void's seeking loop run without visual support. The player has been developing audio-only orientation for the entire journey. Here that skill is required in its pure form: the universe offers nothing to look at. The signal is all there is.

The warmth seeking runs simultaneously: the thermal gradient tells the player where the safe zone is relative to where the signal is pointing. Managing both at once — signal direction versus thermal safety — is the stage's specific challenge.

See `mechanics/seeking_mechanic.md`.

---

## The Pioneer

This Pioneer is the oldest. No medium preserved it — not ice, not sulfur, not methane. On a rogue planet with no atmosphere and no active geology, nothing changes. Nothing weathers. Nothing buries. The Pioneer has been sitting on this surface in vacuum for billions of years and is exactly as it was when it stopped: no decay, no erosion, no burial.

It looks new. It has been here since before humanity existed — since before complex life on Earth — and it looks like it arrived recently. The player understands something about vacuum preservation, and then understands what the Pioneer's age actually implies, and both recognitions arrive in sequence.

Time is different here. This is the stage that makes that most literal.

---

## Ghost Behavior

Ghost ships on this surface persist indefinitely. Nothing moves them. Nothing buries them. Nothing degrades them. This is the one planetary stage where ghost accumulation approaches what the void offers: every death is still exactly where it happened, perfectly preserved.

Over many visits: a surface covered in ghost ships, each one new-looking, each one exactly where it fell. The planet does not forget and does not change. It simply holds everything that has stopped here.

**Resource:** thermal residue — same thermal charge mechanic as the ice moon. Same charge system.

See `mechanics/ghosts.md`.

## Return: Memory Fragment

*Trigger: total darkness, solitude that is not lonely*

Two houses on the same property. At night, from the smaller front house, lights visible in the back house. Not close enough to hear anything. Close enough to know someone was there. The specific thing that is not lonely — proximity without intrusion, each inside their own light.

## Atmosphere

**Visual:**

The ship lights are the only light source in the universe here. They illuminate twenty meters of rock surface and nothing else. The horizon is a line — barely, in the faint starlight — but nothing is visible beyond it. The Milky Way is a smear above, beautiful and useless as navigation.

Every ghost ship looks new. Accumulated across visits: a field of wrecked ships, none of them damaged by time, each one sitting exactly where it stopped. The planet holds them perfectly. They should look ancient but cannot. The disconnect between their apparent freshness and the player's accumulating understanding of how long they have been there is the stage's defining strangeness.

The sky above is the most star-dense of any stage — the rogue planet is in open interstellar space, not inside a system where one star washes out the others. Every star is visible. All of them useless.

**Audio:**

Absolute silence except ship sounds. No wind. No geology. No EM environment. The ship's systems are the only sound in the universe here. The cosmic signal, when the player is oriented correctly, comes out of that silence with unusual clarity — there is nothing to compete with it.

The thermal sensing is subtle: a very low hum that shifts slightly in character as the hull temperature changes. The player learns to read the hum without explicit instruction.
