# Stage — The Rogue

*No star. No sun. No light from above. This world has been alone in the dark for billions of years.*

---

## Setting

A rogue planet — ejected from its solar system an immense time ago, now drifting through interstellar space with no star to orbit. The surface has no sunlight. The sky is not the black of the void with its field of navigating stars: the stars are there, impossibly distant, the Milky Way a faint smear, but no single star close enough to matter. This is a different quality of darkness from anything the player has encountered. The void was empty space between things. This is a world that exists in darkness, has always existed in darkness, will always exist in darkness.

Beneath the surface: residual formation heat and slow radioactive decay keep a thin region warmer than absolute zero. The planet is not dead. It is alone.

The player navigates across a dark surface lit only by their ship's lights. The lights reach perhaps twenty meters. Beyond that: dark.

---

## Mode

Free-roam. The scale of the surface is planetary but the player navigates a bounded region — not bounded by walls but by thermal necessity. Moving too far from the warm zone risks hull freeze. The warm zone is detectable but poorly localized: warmth radiated from below through the surface, diffuse, gradient rather than point source.

The cosmic signal is detectable — no atmosphere to block it, no significant magnetosphere to attenuate it. The signal is here. But finding its direction is harder: the void had stars to triangulate against, a field of context. The rogue planet has only ground and horizon and the twenty-meter radius of ship light. Direction must be established purely from the signal itself, with no visual reference to anchor it.

---

## Threat — Cold

Hull temperature, as on the ice moon. But without clear vent points to seek. The warmth here radiates from below, through the surface, everywhere slightly and more warm in some directions than others. Gradient navigation: the seeking mechanic for warmth, but diffuse — more like reading the temperature of a large room than following a vent.

This stage is slower and more patient than the ice moon. The ice moon had sharp vent points. Here the signal for warmth is everywhere and subtle. The player develops sensitivity to small differences.

---

## The Ghost_Reacher

This ghost_reacher is the oldest. No medium preserved it — not ice, not sulfur, not methane. On a rogue planet with no atmosphere and no active geology, nothing changes. Nothing weathers. Nothing buries. The ghost_reacher has been sitting on this surface in vacuum for billions of years and is exactly as it was when it stopped: no decay, no erosion, no burial.

It looks new. It has been here since before humanity existed — since before complex life on Earth — and it looks like it arrived recently. The player understands something about vacuum preservation, and then understands what the ghost_reacher's age actually implies, and both recognitions arrive in sequence.

Time is different here. This is the stage that makes that most literal.

---

## Ghost Behavior

Ghost ships on this surface persist indefinitely. Nothing moves them. Nothing buries them. Nothing degrades them. This is the one planetary stage where ghost accumulation approaches what the void offers: every death is still exactly where it happened, perfectly preserved.

Over many visits: a surface covered in ghost ships, each one new-looking, each one exactly where it fell. The planet does not forget and does not change. It simply holds everything that has stopped here.

**Resource:** thermal residue — same thermal charge mechanic as the ice moon. Same charge system.

---

## Return Memory Trigger

Total darkness, and a world that has been alone in it for longer than any human reckoning. On the return journey this surfaces a memory of solitude specifically — not loneliness, not suffering. The specific quality of being alone inside something and finding it sufficient. A memory of solitude that was chosen, or accepted, or simply was. The dark that felt like a room rather than an absence.

The player has been to the Colossus and back. They understand something about what it means to exist at scale without witness. The rogue planet reflects it back: a world that simply is, without audience, without sun, without end. The memory that surfaces here is the one closest to understanding why the journey was made.

## Open

- Whether there is any ambient light at all — very faint starlight, just enough to see the horizon as a line
- How to render total darkness on N64 given hardware constraints — what the ship lights actually illuminate, falloff curve
- The ghost_reacher's age: whether there is any in-game way to communicate billions of years without stating it
- Whether this is the most visually minimalist stage in the game — the simplest, emptiest, most patient
- Sound design: absolute silence except ship sounds, which become the only sound in the universe here
